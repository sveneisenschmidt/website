Last week left me feeling that even a good camera kit doesn't guarantee inspiration or good photos. I was looking forward to today's trip to break the loop of dissatisfying pictures.

<!--more-->

Today the [NABU Südraum Leipzig](https://suedraum-leipzig.nabu-sachsen.de/index.php?article_id=17) held its annual guided bird song hike, a good chance to put the Sigma TC 1401 to work on the Canon 1D-X Mark II with the Sigma 150-600mm f/5-6.3. That puts the effective focal length at 840mm. [Nadine](https://www.instagram.com/snapped.in.pixels/) brought her Canon 2000D and Canon EF-S 55-250 f/4-5.6.

**My takeaway:** the Sigma TC 1401 only makes sense in specific situations, plenty of light and stationary subjects. In the forest the Canon 1D-X Mark II pushed the ISO into the thousands to compensate for the narrower aperture. AF response drops from around 100ms to around 300ms. I shrugged the effect off at first but it is constant and seriously hurts focus at the full 840mm, locking onto subjects gets hard. Sunk cost fallacy at its best. The Canon's subject tracking loses reliability too. I did not expect that.

The Sigma TC 1401 will have a place in my kit but not for bird photography under challenging circumstances. It works best with plenty of light and stationary subjects. It likely works also with less good light and stationary subjects as as long I adjust the exposire via a lower shutter speed. I cannot recommend it as a daily driver to push the lens beyond 600mm because there is no way to mitigate the functional penalties like the slower AF and reliability in subject tracking. Just save the money and get an 800mm in the first place.
